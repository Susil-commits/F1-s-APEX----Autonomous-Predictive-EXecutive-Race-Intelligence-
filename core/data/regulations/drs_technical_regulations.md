# FIA Technical & Sporting Regulations — Article 21 & 3.10.10: Drag Reduction System (DRS)

## Overview
The Drag Reduction System (DRS) is a driver-controllable aerodynamic device designed to reduce rear wing profile drag, facilitating overtaking on designated straight-line acceleration sectors.

## Relevant Strategy Levers
- `sector_1_delta_s`: Sector 1 delta to pole (acceleration/straightline pace).
- `sector_2_delta_s`: Sector 2 delta to pole (technical downforce vs speed).
- `sector_3_delta_s`: Sector 3 delta to pole (long straightline power speed).
- `sector_1_delta_norm`, `sector_2_delta_norm`, `sector_3_delta_norm`: Normalized sector pace deltas.

## Key Regulatory Clauses
1. **Technical Regulations Article 3.10.10 (Flap Geometric Bounds)**: When DRS is activated, the rear wing upper flap moves to create a slot gap between 10mm (closed position) and a maximum of 85mm (open position). Any opening exceeding 85mm breaches technical regulations and results in immediate qualifying disqualification.
2. **Sporting Regulations Article 21.1 (Detection and Gap Threshold)**: DRS may only be activated within designated activation zones if the driver is within 1.000 second of the leading car at the preceding electronic timing detection point.
3. **Sporting Regulations Article 21.2 (Race Neutralization & Start)**: DRS is disabled for the first lap of the race following the start or any Safety Car restart period.
4. **Sporting Regulations Article 21.3 (Wet Weather Suspension)**: The Race Director will immediately disable DRS across all zones if intermediate or wet weather tyres are fitted, or if visibility and track surface water are deemed unsafe.
